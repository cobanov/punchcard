Privacy Policy

Last updated 10 August 2026

helva is a todo app. It stores the tasks you write and the account you sign in with, and nothing else. There is no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no third-party SDK inside the app.

Who runs this

The hosted service at todo.cobanov.run is operated by Mert Cobanov. For any question about this policy or about your data, write to mertcobanov@gmail.com.

helva is also open to self-hosting. If you are using someone else's helva server, this policy describes the software's behaviour but the operator of that server is the one holding your data.

What is stored

  • Your email address. It identifies the account and is where account email is sent.
  • Your password, hashed. Never the password itself. If you sign in with Google or with Apple instead, an identifier from that provider is stored in its place so we can recognise you next time.
  • A display name and a profile picture, if you choose to set them. Both are optional.
  • Your content: list names, task titles, notes, due dates, and their order.
  • Sign-in records: active sessions and any API tokens you create, so you can see and revoke them.
  • A small security log of account events such as signing in, changing a password, or deleting the account, together with the IP address the request came from. It exists so you can tell whether someone else has been in your account.
  • A two-factor secret, only if you turn two-factor authentication on.

What is not

  • No advertising identifiers, and nothing is shared with advertisers.
  • No analytics or usage tracking, on the web or in the apps.
  • No location, contacts, photos, calendar or health data.
  • Nothing is sold, and nothing is shared with anyone for their own purposes.

Why it is stored

To run the service you asked for: to keep you signed in, to sync your tasks between your devices, to share a list with someone when you invite them, and to let you get back into your account if you lose your password. The security log exists to protect the account. There is no other purpose.

Who else sees it

  • People you share a list with. They see that list, its tasks, and the name or email you are identified by. Nothing else.
  • Your sign-in provider, if you use one. Signing in with Google or Apple tells them you signed in to helva. helva receives only an identifier and your email address from them.
  • Webhooks you configure yourself. If you point a webhook at another service, helva sends that service what you told it to. That is your integration and your choice.
  • Nobody else. There are no third-party processors beyond the server hosting itself.

Where it lives, and for how long

On the server running helva, in a PostgreSQL database, for as long as your account exists. Deleted tasks are kept briefly so that a device which was offline can still learn they were deleted, and are then removed.

Getting your data out

Settings → Data → Export my data gives you a JSON file containing every list and task the account can reach, including ones you have deleted, plus your account details. It is a complete copy and it is available at any time without asking anyone.

Deleting your account

Settings → Data → Delete account, inside the app. The deletion is immediate and permanent — your account, your lists, your tasks, your sessions and your tokens are removed from the database, not flagged as hidden. It cannot be undone, which is why it asks you to type DELETE first.

One thing has to happen first: if you own a list you have shared with someone else, helva will ask you to hand it over or stop sharing it before it will delete the account, so that other people's work does not disappear with yours.

Children

helva is not directed at children and does not knowingly hold data from anyone under 13.

Changes

If this policy changes in a way that affects what is collected or who sees it, the date at the top changes and the change is described in the app's release notes.
